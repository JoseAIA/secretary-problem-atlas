Research Timeline

A History of the Secretary Problem

From dynamic programming and the (1/e) rule to modern strategic and algorithmic variants.

This curated timeline highlights foundational, influential, and especially relevant contributions. It is not intended as an exhaustive bibliography or an exact citation ranking.

More than sixty years of optimal stopping

The secretary problem developed from a recreational question into a major model in optimal stopping, applied probability, decision theory, economics, and online algorithms.

1961 Foundation

Dynamic programming enters the problem

D. V. Lindley

Lindley gave the first published scientific treatment commonly recognized as solving the classical problem, using dynamic programming and backward induction.

Dynamic Programming and Decision Theory

1964 Classical model

The relative-rank formulation

Y. S. Chow, S. Moriguti, H. Robbins and S. M. Samuels

A rigorous analysis of optimal selection when only the relative ranks of the observations are available.

Optimum Selection Based on Relative Rank

1966 Landmark

The definitive foundational paper

John P. Gilbert and Frederick Mosteller

This highly influential work unified several best-choice models, including the classical no-information problem and the full-information problem.

Recognizing the Maximum of a Sequence

View publication
1979 Multiple choice

Beyond selecting only the maximum

Mitsushi Tamaki

Tamaki studied a multiple-choice objective involving recognition of both the maximum and the second maximum.

Recognizing Both the Maximum and the Second Maximum of a Sequence

1983 Survey

The first major synthesis

P. R. Freeman

Freeman organized the rapidly expanding literature and reviewed the classical problem together with its principal extensions.

The Secretary Problem and Its Extensions: A Review

View publication
1984 Generalization

A unified approach and the 1/e law

F. Thomas Bruss

Bruss developed a unified continuous-time approach for best-choice problems in which the number of available options may be unknown.

A Unified Approach to a Class of Best Choice Problems with an Unknown Number of Options

View publication
1989 History

Who solved the secretary problem?

Thomas S. Ferguson

Ferguson reconstructed the problem’s complicated origins, distinguishing its unpublished history, recreational appearances, and formal mathematical solutions.

Who Solved the Secretary Problem?

View publication
2000 Landmark

The odds theorem

F. Thomas Bruss

The odds theorem gives an elegant rule for maximizing the probability of stopping on the last success in a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials.

Sum the Odds to One and Stop

View publication
2005 Algorithms

The algorithmic secretary problem

Robert Kleinberg

The multiple-choice secretary problem connected classical optimal stopping with online algorithms, approximation guarantees, and online auctions.

A Multiple-Choice Secretary Algorithm with Applications to Online Auctions

2012 Applicant strategy

The applicant’s point of view

Darren B. Glass

Instead of asking when the employer should stop, Glass asks which interview position an applicant should choose to maximize the probability of being hired.

The Secretary Problem from the Applicant’s Point of View

View publication

RESEARCH BY THE ATLAS AUTHOR

Contributions by José A. Islas

These papers develop secretary-type and optimal-stopping problems involving non-identically distributed observations, approximate selection, random walks, and strategic interview positioning.

2016 Peer reviewed

A Sharp Lower Bound for Choosing the Maximum of an Independent Sequence

Pieter C. Allaart and José A. Islas

Establishes a sharp distribution-free lower bound for selecting the maximum from independent observations with possibly different continuous distributions.

Journal of Applied Probability, 53(4), 1041–1051

Journal arXiv
2018 Peer reviewed

A Sharp Bound for Winning Within a Proportion of the Maximum of a Sequence

José A. Islas

Studies the problem of stopping at an observation whose value is at least a fixed proportion of the overall maximum and derives a sharp lower bound for the optimal success probability.

Boletín de la Sociedad Matemática Mexicana, 25, 737–746

Journal arXiv
2024 Peer reviewed

Oscillation Criteria for Stopping Near the Top of a Random Walk

José A. Islas

Investigates optimal stopping at one of the two highest values of a finite Bernoulli random walk and establishes new properties and bounds for the associated critical constants.

Boletín de la Sociedad Matemática Mexicana, 30, article 43

Journal arXiv
2026 Preprint

Strategic Interview Positioning under Uncertain Self-Rank

José A. Islas

Introduces a truncated geometric prior for an applicant’s unknown absolute rank and identifies an explicit phase transition between the first eligible and final interview positions.
